# English AI Slop Pattern Library

Used together with `SKILL.md`. Every pattern is tagged with its root cause (R1–R5, defined in SKILL.md section 5).

---

## R3 — Banned & Hollow Buzzwords (cut or replace on sight)

`delve`, `foster`, `leverage` (as a verb for "use"), `utilize`, `facilitate`, `empower`, `streamline`, `robust`, `cutting-edge`, `paradigm shift`, `game changer`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `beacon`, `multifaceted`, `meticulous`, `intricate`, `paramount`, `transformative`, `elevate`, `embark`, `supercharge`, `harness`, `ever-evolving`, `testament to`.

## R1 — Empty Openers, Closers & Throat-Clearing

- **Throat-clearing openers**: "In today's fast-paced digital world," "It is important to remember that," "At the end of the day," "When it comes to X," "The reality is."
- **Empty adverbs**: `fundamentally`, `crucially`, `inherently`, `literally`, `importantly` — cut unless they mark a genuine contrast.
- **Hollow sign-offs**: generic thank-you / well-wishing closers that add no content.

## R2 — Formulaic Structure & Rhetorical Tics

Flag when clustered (≥2 in a short stretch) or clearly formulaic, same clustering/portability test as the Chinese library:

1. **Binary Contrast Stacks**: "This isn't just about X; it's about Y." → State Y directly.
2. **Faux-Insight Setups**: "Here's the thing:", "What most people get wrong about X:", "Plot twist:" → Cut the setup, state the insight.
3. **Colon Reveals for Fake Drama**: "The secret behind their success: a simple checklist." → Write as one clean sentence.
4. **Dramatic Micro-Fragmentation**: "Scale. Speed. Power. That's it." → Use complete, natural sentences.
5. **Fake-Profound & Recap Endings**: mic-drop metaphors, "In conclusion," "Ultimately," "To sum up." → End on the actual last concrete takeaway.

## R4 — Fake Logic & Surface Analysis

- **Trailing participle tags** that mimic analysis without adding it: "...highlighting their commitment to innovation, showcasing their vision." → State the concrete outcome or cut the clause.
- **Unjustified connectors**: "therefore," "as a result," "it becomes clear that" used where no real causal link exists → cut, or replace with the actual mechanism.
- **False balance**: "on one hand... on the other hand..." with no stance ever taken → make the author's actual position explicit, or cut the hedge.

## R5 — Visual & Formatting Tics

- Em dashes used as an omnipresent rhythm crutch across short paragraphs → use standard commas and periods.
- Random bolding on mid-sentence keywords → remove.
- Headers/bullets imposed on what should be plain prose paragraphs → collapse back into prose.

## Voice Sanctuary notes specific to English text

Beyond the general rules in SKILL.md section 4:

- Sentence fragments used deliberately for emphasis by the author (not the AI dramatic-fragmentation pattern above) are a legitimate stylistic choice if they appear once, tied to a specific beat — don't over-correct a single fragment into a full sentence.
- Regional or industry-specific idiom that reads as slightly "off" but is the author's actual voice is not the same as AI jargon — check before cutting.
